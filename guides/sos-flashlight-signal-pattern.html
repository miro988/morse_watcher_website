How to Flash SOS with a Flashlight: Emergency Timing & Pattern

In an emergency, optical signals cut through total darkness and penetrate distances where screams and whistles cannot reach. The international distress signal SOS (• • • — — — • • •) is universally recognized across search and rescue teams, pilots, and maritime vessels worldwide.

1. The Visual SOS Pattern

SOS is not three separate words; in Morse signaling, it is transmitted as a single continuous prosign:

• • •   — — —   • • •

3 Short Flashes  •  3 Long Flashes  •  3 Short Flashes

2. Exact Pulse & Pause Timing

The difference between an intentional SOS and an erratic blinking light is the timing ratio. Rescuers look for regular periodicity:

Element Light State Standard Duration
3 Dots (S) ON (short) ~0.3–0.5 sec each (1 unit pause between)
Inter-letter pause OFF ~1.0–1.5 sec (3 units)
3 Dashes (O) ON (long) ~1.0–1.5 sec each (1 unit pause between)
Inter-letter pause OFF ~1.0–1.5 sec (3 units)
3 Dots (S) ON (short) ~0.3–0.5 sec each
Cycle Repeat Pause OFF ~5.0–7.0 sec (full cycle pause before repeating)

3. Critical Signaling Tips for Search & Rescue

  1. Aim at Reflective Surfaces or Ridges: If you see a search plane, helicopter, or boat, sweep the beam across their cockpit or bridge. If signaling someone below, bounce light off rock faces or water.
  2. The Repeat Pause Matters: Never flash continuously without pausing. The 5–7 second dark interval gives observers time to turn their heads, confirm what they saw, and anticipate the next burst.
  3. Conserve Battery Power: Do not leave the flashlight on full beam. Strobing SOS uses significantly less battery than continuous flood illumination.
